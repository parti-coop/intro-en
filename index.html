Parti Co-op · Seoul, South Korea

A digital square where citizens dialogue and take action.

Parti is a social platform cooperative — led by its crew members, sustained by citizen supporters. For over a decade we have been building and running digital civic squares and deliberation platforms in Korea, trying to carry citizens' voices into real decisions. Our aim is to grow into an anchor institution for digital democracy in Korea.

Individuals speak, talk, and act — and through that, they feel efficacy, trust their community, and become citizens.

How we work

The practice came first, not the tools

For over a decade Parti has convened up to twenty offline deliberations, living labs, and civic workshops every month — conservatively, 1,000+ field events. Every platform we built grew out of what we first learned in real rooms with real citizens.

Trust · rules · stewardship

A civic platform works when trust, procedural rules, and a responsible operating body are built together with the code — a combination we have been learning to get right for ten years, alongside the institutions that use DemosX.

Citizen ownership, by structure

Parti is a cooperative: the square is owned by the people who use and run it. Governments adopt our platforms, but the ownership stays with citizens — that independence has held across changes of administration.

Knowledge as a public good

We publish our deliberation playbooks, formats, and operating guides — including deliberative programs such as Galaxy Voting, Star-to-Star Dialogue, and Policy Battles — so others can adapt them. toolkit.parti.coop

Right now

What we're working on, mid-2026

A snapshot of the square and the institutions it connects to — the work that fills our weeks at the moment.

A story from the square

When a middle-school newspaper was suspended by its principal, 30,000 citizens joined a campaign on the square — signing and donating — to defend the students' right to publish. The square matters most clearly when it protects the smallest public voices.

On the square

The square, day to day

Up to 180,000 citizens a month use the square to campaign, vote, deliberate, fact-check, form circles, and follow civic news. Parti FactCheck is in the final stage of IFCN certification, and about twenty offline deliberations a month run alongside ongoing civic-tech projects.

Our deliberative formats keep traveling: Galaxy Voting and Star-to-Star Dialogue with Hanyang University students on unification and peace — and most recently, a Star-to-Star Dialogue with visiting students from India, on Korea's social issues.

With institutions

One pattern across ten-plus public bodies

We currently work with more than ten ministries, local governments, and public bodies — different institutions, one pattern: proposal → deliberation → policy as continuing infrastructure. With the presidential National Unity Commission, citizens set the agenda for social cohesion — 4,000+ opinions gathered, then public deliberation on the agendas citizens chose. With the National Education Commission, a 500-citizen committee runs a year-long program close to a citizens' assembly. With the Ministry of the Interior and Safety, we co-host "Everyone's Discussion" — public forums of around 200 citizens each. With the Economic, Social and Labor Council, Star-to-Star Dialogue runs between stakeholders. With the Ministry of Unification, a proof-of-concept on AI-supported public deliberation is underway — and through it we are upgrading our Galaxy Voting and Chorus modules. On the Basic Plan for Employment Stability in Industrial Transition, we gathered citizen input through Galaxy Voting and convened a multi-stakeholder roundtable with the Ministry of Employment and Labor; traveling deliberations with Seoul, Busan, and other education offices take on AI in education and admissions reform.

Context

Thirty years of digital democracy in Korea

How does a society carry the energy of its plazas into everyday institutions? Korea has lived that question in a compressed form — repeated crises, massive candlelight plazas, and each time, an attempt to turn that energy into durable civic infrastructure. Parti grew up inside this history, as one thread among many. Thirty years in one line: digital civic squares and digital participation policies co-evolved — toward more, better, and everyday democracy. For the full story, read our chair's essay: A Genealogy of Korean Digital Democracy.

1990s · Emergence

PC networks — the first online public squares

Long before broadband, dial-up PC network services — Hitel, Chollian, Nownuri — ran crowded debate boards where thousands of citizens argued public issues online for the first time. Korean online civic culture emerged here.

2000 – 2002 · Expansion

Citizens become the media

OhmyNews launched in 2000 with the motto "every citizen is a reporter." In 2002, the first candlelight vigil was called through an online post — the internet had become a civic force of its own.

2005

Government answers online — e-People

The government consolidated petitions and complaints into a single online channel, e-People — the start of an institutional lineage that later ran through the National Petition platform, Gwanghwamun 1st Avenue, and Democracy Seoul.

2004 – 2008

Agora — the first mass online public square

Daum's "Agora" forum became the largest online public square of 2000s Korea, drawing more than a million people a day at its peak. It showed that ordinary citizens would deliberate at scale — and it is where Parti's story begins: our chair worked on Agora before leaving to build squares that citizens themselves could own.

2008

Candlelight protests — the online square moves to the street

The protests against US beef imports were organized substantially through online forums, an early landmark of internet-mediated civic action.

2008 protests Candlelight rallies in Korea
2015 · Experimentation

Parti is founded

Developers and activists from the portal-era squares founded Parti Demos, on a conviction the portals could not deliver: the digital civic square should belong to citizens. In 2018 it was incorporated as a social cooperative.

parti.coop
2016 – 2017

The candlelight plazas and a peaceful transfer of power

Millions gathered weekly through a winter of candlelight demonstrations, leading to a presidential impeachment entirely within constitutional process. The question the plazas left behind — how to make participation part of everyday life — has shaped Korean civic tech since.

2016–2017 protests
2017 –

Participation platforms enter government

The post-impeachment government opened the National Petition platform — 200,000 signatures guaranteed an official answer — and "Gwanghwamun 1st Avenue" for citizen proposals, while Seoul launched "Democracy Seoul." Parti helped build and operate these platforms — for us, the beginning of the civic collaboration model that later became DemosX.

demosx.org
2019

Facing the Ocean — East Asian civic tech joins hands

Civic hackers from Korea, Japan, and Taiwan began meeting and hacking together as Facing the Ocean — a network Parti helped propose and organize with g0v and Code for Japan, starting in Okinawa 2019.

Code for Japan Taiwan Insight essay g0v Summit review
2020

COVID-19 — civic hackers and open data in 3 days

When mask shortages hit, Parti proposed a joint civic-tech response for public data. The government opened pharmacy inventory data; within 3 days citizen developers shipped the first apps — 200+ volunteers, 50+ free apps. The collaboration grew into Code for Korea.

codefor.kr
2022 – 2024 · Inflection

Deliberation becomes infrastructure

DemosX spread across central ministries, metropolitan government, education offices, and the presidential National Unity Commission — the same civic collaboration model, adopted as continuing infrastructure.

Dec 2024

The martial law crisis — and citizens, again

A sudden martial law declaration was overturned within hours by the National Assembly, surrounded and protected by citizens; impeachment and a peaceful election followed. Korea's democracy held — and the crisis renewed the case that democratic capacity must live in everyday infrastructure.

2024 martial law crisis
2025 – 2026

AI meets deliberation

Chorus, our agenda-discovery module, uses AI to sort tens of thousands of citizen opinions while human moderators keep judgment in the loop, so that citizens themselves surface the agenda — first deployed with the presidential National Unity Commission. Parti designs AI-supported one-on-one deep dialogue and public forums with the Ministry of Unification, and our chair serves on the AI Democracy Division of the presidential National AI Strategy Council.
